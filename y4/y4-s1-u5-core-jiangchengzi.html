1 《江jiāng城chéng子zi·密mì州zhōu出chū猎liè》是shì北běi宋sòng著zhù名míng文wén学xué家jiā苏sū轼shì创chuàng作zuò的de一yī首shǒu词cí。这zhè首shǒu词cí风fēng格gé豪háo放fàng，在zài当dāng时shí的de词cí坛tán上shàng显xiǎn得de别bié具jù一yī格gé，与yǔ众zhòng不bù同tóng。
1"To the Tune of Jiang Cheng Zi: Hunting at Mizhou" is a ci poem by the celebrated Northern Song writer Su Shi. Bold and unrestrained in style, it stood apart from anything else on the ci-poetry scene of its day.
2 苏sū轼shì，姓xìng苏sū名míng轼shì，字zì子zi瞻zhān，但dàn人rén们men一yì般bān称chēng他tā为wèi苏sū东dōng坡pō，这zhè来lái源yuán于yú他tā的de号hào“东dōng坡pō居jū士shì”。他tā曾céng经jīng因yīn为wèi与yǔ朝cháo中zhōng的de当dāng权quán者zhě政zhèng见jiàn不bù同tóng而ér被bèi贬biǎn到dào黄huáng州zhōu（今jīn湖hú北běi黄huáng冈gāng）。由yóu于yú生shēng活huó贫pín困kùn，缺quē少shǎo粮liáng食shí，他tā不bù得dé不bù带dài着zhe家jiā人rén在zài黄huáng州zhōu东dōng面miàn的de山shān坡pō上shàng开kāi荒huāng种zhòng田tián，他tā的de号hào“东dōng坡pō”便biàn由yóu此cǐ而ér来lái。苏sū轼shì学xué识shí广guǎng博bó，多duō才cái多duō艺yì。他tā不bù但dàn创chuàng作zuò了le许xǔ多duō流liú传chuán千qiān古gǔ的de诗shī词cí和hé文wén章zhāng，而ér且qiě在zài书shū法fǎ和hé绘huì画huà方fāng面miàn都dōu取qǔ得dé了le极jí高gāo的de成chéng就jiù。苏sū轼shì是shì一yí个gè乐lè观guān的de人rén，从cóng未wèi被bèi生shēng活huó的de磨mó难nàn所suǒ压yā倒dǎo。即jí使shǐ身shēn处chù困kùn境jìng，他tā仍réng然rán能néng够gòu以yǐ豁huò达dá的de心xīn胸xiōng面miàn对duì人rén生shēng的de风fēng风fēng雨yǔ雨yǔ。
2Su Shi — surname Su, given name Shi, courtesy name Zizhan — is more commonly known to people as Su Dongpo, after his self-chosen name "Recluse of the Eastern Slope" (东坡居士). He was once banished to Huangzhou (modern-day Huanggang in Hubei) because his political views clashed with those holding power at court. Because life there was poor and food short, he had no choice but to bring his family to clear and farm a slope of land east of Huangzhou — and that is where his name "Eastern Slope" came from. Su Shi was a man of vast learning and many talents. He created poems and essays that have endured through the ages, and reached the highest levels in calligraphy and painting as well. Su Shi was an optimistic man, never crushed by life's hardships. Even in the most difficult circumstances, he could face the storms of life with a broad and magnanimous heart.
3 虽suī然rán在zài官guān场chǎng上shàng不bù断duàn受shòu到dào排pái挤jǐ和hé打dǎ压yā，但dàn苏sū轼shì并bìng未wèi因yīn此cǐ而ér变biàn得dé消xiāo沉chén，他tā始shǐ终zhōng怀huái有yǒu报bào效xiào国guó家jiā的de满mǎn腔qiāng热rè忱chén。公gōng元yuán1074年nián，他tā被bèi派pài往wǎng往wǎng密mì州zhōu（今jīn山shān东dōng诸zhū城chéng）担dān任rèn当dāng地dì的de地dì方fāng官guān。第dì二èr年nián，他tā因yīn天tiān气qì干gān旱hàn而ér去qù当dāng地dì的de山shān上shàng主zhǔ持chí求qiú雨yǔ的de仪yí式shì。返fǎn回huí时shí，他tā与yǔ众zhòng人rén在zài郊jiāo外wài打dǎ了le一yī次cì猎liè，并bìng将jiāng打dǎ猎liè的de经jīng过guò写xiě成chéng了le一yī首shǒu词cí——《江jiāng城chéng子zi · 密mì州zhōu出chū猎liè》：
3Though pushed aside and pressed down in officialdom over and over, Su Shi did not let himself sink into despondency — he carried, always, a heart full of fervent devotion to serve his country. In 1074 CE, he was sent to Mizhou (modern-day Zhucheng in Shandong) to serve as the local magistrate. The following year, due to a drought, he went to a nearby mountain to preside over a rain-summoning ceremony. On the way back, he and his retinue went hunting in the countryside — and he turned the experience into a ci poem, "To the Tune of Jiang Cheng Zi: Hunting at Mizhou":

4 老lǎo夫fū聊liáo发fā少shào年nián狂kuáng，左zuǒ牵qiān黄huáng，右yòu擎qíng苍cāng，锦jǐn帽mào貂diāo裘qiú①，千qiān骑qí卷juǎn平píng冈gāng。为wèi报bào倾qīng城chéng②随suí太tài守shǒu，亲qīn射shè虎hǔ，看kàn孙sūn郎láng。
4This old man, just for the moment, lets his youth run wild — In his left hand, a yellow hound; on his right wrist, a hawk dark as steel. In embroidered cap and sable furs,① a thousand horsemen sweep across the level ridge. To repay the whole town②for following their magistrate out today, I shall shoot the tiger with my own hand, as Master Sun once did.

5 酒jiǔ酣hān胸xiōng胆dǎn尚shàng开kāi张zhāng③，鬓bìn微wēi霜shuāng，又yòu何hé妨fáng？持chí节jié④云yún中zhōng⑤，何hé日rì遣qiǎn冯féng唐táng⑥？会huì挽wǎn雕diāo弓gōng如rú满mǎn月yuè，西xī北běi望wàng，射shè天tiān狼láng⑦。
5Wine flowing, my heart and courage open ever wider;③ the frost at my temples — what does it matter? With the imperial token in hand,④bound for Yunzhong⑤— when will the court send forth a Feng Tang for me?⑥ I shall draw my carved bow taut as the full moon, and looking northwest, take aim at the Heavenly Wolf.⑦

6① Embroidered cap and sable furs: dressed in resplendent attire. ② The whole town: every person of the town, signifying a great crowd. ③ Heart and courage open ever wider: the heart grows broader, the courage grows bolder. ④ Imperial token: the credentials carried by a messenger of the emperor in ancient times. ⑤ Yunzhong: a place name on the northern frontier during the Qin and Han. ⑥ Feng Tang: a high official of the Western Han who, as the emperor's special envoy, once travelled to Yunzhong to lift a punishment imposed on Governor Wei Shang. ⑦ Heavenly Wolf: refers to Sirius (the Wolf Star); here, it stands for the invaders from the northwest.
7 这zhè首shǒu词cí分fēn上shàng下xià两liǎng片piàn，上shàng片piàn写xiě事shì，下xià片piàn抒shū情qíng。整zhěng首shǒu词cí气qì势shì豪háo迈mài，慷kāng慨kǎi激jī昂áng。苏sū轼shì虽suī然rán自zì称chēng“老lǎo夫fū”，但dàn在zài词cí中zhōng表biǎo现xiàn出chū的de是shì一yī种zhǒng年nián轻qīng人rén的de豪háo放fàng与yǔ激jī情qíng。他tā一yī副fù猎liè人rén的de装zhuāng扮bàn，带dài着zhe黄huáng狗gǒu、苍cāng鹰yīng，与yǔ大dà队duì人rén马mǎ在zài平píng坦tǎn的de山shān冈gāng上shàng追zhuī逐zhú猎liè物wù。他tā勉miǎn励lì自zì己jǐ：要yào像xiàng三sān国guó时shí的de孙sūn权quán那nà样yàng，射shè杀shā猛měng虎hǔ，一yī展zhǎn雄xióng姿zī。
7This ci poem is divided into upper and lower stanzas — the upper recounts the events, the lower gives voice to feeling. The whole poem rings with bold, dashing spirit and an impassioned, surging energy. Though Su Shi calls himself an "old man," what comes through in the lines is the bold abandon and passion of a youth. Dressed as a hunter, with his yellow hound and dark hawk, he rides with a great company across the level ridge in pursuit of game. He spurs himself on: to be like Sun Quan of the Three Kingdoms — to fell the fierce tiger himself, and show his heroic mettle.
8 虽suī然rán苏sū轼shì已yǐ经jīng不bù算suàn年nián轻qīng，但dàn他tā觉jué得de自zì己jǐ胆dǎn气qì豪háo壮zhuàng，两liǎng鬓bìn的de白bái发fà并bìng不bù影yǐng响xiǎng他tā为wèi国guó效xiào力lì。他tā举jǔ了le一yí个gè典diǎn故gù：西xī汉hàn时shí，云yún中zhōng郡jùn太tài守shǒu魏wèi尚shàng因yīn为wèi犯fàn错cuò而ér被bèi治zhì罪zuì，后hòu来lái朝cháo廷tíng接jiē受shòu了le冯féng唐táng的de意yì见jiàn，让ràng魏wèi尚shàng官guān复fù原yuán职zhí。苏sū轼shì希xī望wàng自zì己jǐ也yě能néng得dé到dào重zhòng用yòng，更gèng希xī望wàng有yǒu朝zhāo一yī日rì能néng够gòu奔bēn赴fù西xī北běi前qián线xiàn，实shí现xiàn杀shā敌dí报bào国guó的de梦mèng想xiǎng。
8Though Su Shi was no longer young, he felt his courage was strong and bold — and the white hair at his temples would not stop him from serving his country. He invokes a classical allusion: in the Western Han, Wei Shang, governor of Yunzhong, was punished for an offence; later the court accepted Feng Tang's counsel and restored Wei Shang to his post. Su Shi hoped he too would be put to use — and hoped, above all, that one day he would ride forth to the northwest frontier and fulfil his dream of striking down the enemy and serving his country.
9 这zhè首shǒu词cí上shàng片piàn描miáo写xiě打dǎ猎liè的de场chǎng景jǐng，下xià片piàn笔bǐ锋fēng一yī转zhuǎn，表biǎo现xiàn出chū要yào保bǎo家jiā卫wèi国guó、建jiàn功gōng立lì业yè的de雄xióng心xīn壮zhuàng志zhì。整zhěng首shǒu词cí气qì势shì雄xióng浑hún，境jìng界jiè开kāi阔kuò，充chōng分fèn体tǐ现xiàn了le苏sū轼shì广guǎng阔kuò的de胸xiōng怀huái与yǔ高gāo远yuǎn的de志zhì向xiàng。
9The upper stanza paints the scene of the hunt; the lower stanza turns sharply with the brush, revealing his lofty ambition to defend home and country and to build his great enterprise. The whole poem is grand and powerful in spirit, vast in its vision — a complete embodiment of Su Shi's broad heart and far-reaching aspirations.
