1 有yǒu一yì天tiān，我wǒ和hé来lái自zì海hǎi外wài的de一yí个gè朋péng友yǒu结jié伴bàn乘chéng地dì铁tiě。地dì铁tiě通tōng道dào里lǐ，一yí个gè老lǎo乞qǐ丐gài半bàn跪guì在zài地dì上shàng，挡dǎng住zhù了le阶jiē梯tī。他tā面miàn前qián的de破pò旧jiù草cǎo帽mào中zhōng，撒sā着zhe零líng星xīng的de钱qián币bì。肮āng脏zāng的de毡zhān布bù下xià像xiàng枪qiāng管guǎn一yì般bān刺cì出chū半bàn截jié腿tuǐ，该gāi长zhǎng脚jiǎo的de地dì方fāng，却què是shì一yī团tuán褐hè色sè的de腐fǔ肉ròu。看kàn到dào这zhè情qíng景jǐng，人rén们men不bù得dé不bù远yuǎn远yuǎn抛pāo下xià点diǎn钱qián，然rán后hòu逃táo也yě似shì地dì躲duǒ开kāi。
1One day, I was taking the metro with a friend from abroad. In the underground passageway, an old beggar was kneeling on the ground, blocking the steps. In a battered straw hat in front of him lay a scattering of coins. From beneath a filthy felt cloth, a half-leg jutted out like the barrel of a rifle — and where a foot should have been, there was a brown lump of rotting flesh. Faced with this sight, passers-by tossed a coin from a distance and slipped away as if escaping.
2 眼yǎn前qián的de这zhè一yí幕mù引yǐn起qǐ了le我wǒ的de深shēn思sī：乞qǐ丐gài把bǎ贫pín穷qióng和hé软ruǎn弱ruò直zhí接jiē显xiǎn露lù出chū来lái。他tā们men赤chì裸luǒ裸luǒ地dì祈qí求qiú别bié人rén的de施shī舍shě，让ràng人rén们men在zài同tóng情qíng之zhī后hòu，产chǎn生shēng了le帮bāng助zhù的de欲yù望wàng，也yě获huò得dé了le施shī舍shě的de喜xǐ悦yuè。
2The scene set me thinking. Beggars expose their poverty and frailty directly. They plead nakedly for others' alms — and in those who pity them, the urge to help arises, along with the small pleasure of giving.
3 朋péng友yǒu走zǒu向xiàng乞qǐ丐gài，我wǒ知zhī趣qù地dì退tuì后hòu了le几jǐ步bù，和hé朋péng友yǒu拉lā开kāi距jù离lí。依yī她tā的de慈cí悲bēi和hé博bó爱ài，无wú论lùn捐juān出chū多duō少shǎo，都dōu是shì心xīn意yì，也yě是shì隐yǐn私sī。出chū乎hū我wǒ意yì料liào的de是shì，她tā走zǒu到dào乞qǐ丐gài面miàn前qián对duì他tā说shuō：“请qǐng您nín让ràng一yī让ràng，不bú要yào阻zǔ挡dǎng通tōng道dào。您nín没méi看kàn到dào人rén们men都dōu绕rào开kāi您nín走zǒu吗ma？这zhè让ràng大dà家jiā多duō不bù方fāng便biàn啊a。”老lǎo人rén抬tái起qǐ半bàn张zhāng脸liǎn，以yǐ沙shā哑yǎ的de声shēng音yīn乞qǐ讨tǎo道dào：“行xíng行xíng好hǎo，太tài太tài，给gěi几jǐ个gè小xiǎo钱qián……”
3My friend walked towards the beggar. I tactfully drew back a few steps, putting some distance between us. Knowing her compassion and her broad heart, however much she gave, it was both kindness and a private matter. To my surprise, my friend walked right up to him and said: "Could you please move aside — don't block the passage. Can't you see how everyone has to walk around you? It's making things very awkward for them." The old man lifted half of his face and croaked: "Have mercy, ma'am — just a few small coins…"
4 朋péng友yǒu坦tǎn然rán走zǒu了le过guò去qù，不bù曾céng放fàng下xià一yī枚méi硬yìng币bì。她tā进jìn入rù地dì铁tiě站zhàn，找zhǎo到dào工gōng作zuò人rén员yuán，说shuō：“通tōng道dào上shàng有yǒu个gè乞qǐ丐gài，妨fáng碍ài了le交jiāo通tōng，请qǐng你nǐ们men劝quàn他tā离lí开kāi。”
4My friend walked calmly past, without leaving so much as a single coin. She went into the station, found a member of staff, and said: "There's a beggar in the passageway obstructing the flow of people. Please ask him to leave."
5 我wǒ无wú声shēng地dì看kàn着zhe这zhè一yī切qiè，心xīn想xiǎng：不bù给gěi钱qián尚shàng能néng理lǐ解jiě，但dàn找zhǎo地dì铁tiě工gōng作zuò人rén员yuán赶gǎn走zǒu乞qǐ丐gài，是shì不bú是shì有yǒu些xiē过guò分fèn？我wǒ忍rěn不bú住zhù替tì她tā找zhǎo理lǐ由yóu，便biàn对duì她tā说shuō：“我wǒ看kàn到dào报bào纸zhǐ上shàng的de报bào道dào，有yǒu些xiē乞qǐ丐gài骗piàn吃chī骗piàn喝hē，肆sì无wú忌jì惮dàn。白bái天tiān在zài街jiē上shàng乞qǐ讨tǎo时shí衣yī衫shān褴lán褛lǚ，晚wǎn上shàng则zé西xī装zhuāng革gé履lǚ地dì去qù餐cān馆guǎn吃chī饭fàn。有yǒu的de干gān脆cuì以yǐ此cǐ为wèi业yè，几jǐ年nián下xià来lái，居jū然rán在zài乡xiāng下xià起qǐ楼lóu造zào屋wū，成chéng了liǎo当dàng地dì首shǒu富fù。想xiǎng必bì你nǐ一yī眼yǎn就jiù看kàn出chū那nà乞qǐ丐gài正zhèng是shì这zhè类lèi人rén吧ba？”
5I watched all of this in silence, thinking: not giving I can understand — but finding the station staff to drive the beggar out, isn't that going a bit far? I couldn't help searching for a justification on her behalf, and said: "I read in the papers about how some beggars cheat people for food and drink, completely brazen about it. By day they beg on the street in tatters; by night they go to restaurants in suits and leather shoes. Some treat it as a profession outright — after a few years, they've built mansions back in their home villages and become the richest folk around. You must have spotted at a glance that this beggar was that sort, right?"
6 朋péng友yǒu笑xiào了le，说shuō：“我wǒ哪nǎ有yǒu这zhè等děng神shén功gōng。你nǐ说shuō的de那nà些xiē事shì例lì我wǒ也yě在zài报bào上shàng看kàn过guò。至zhì于yú刚gāng才cái这zhè个ge老lǎo人rén，没méi有yǒu证zhèng据jù，我wǒ们men不bù可kě以yǐ随suí便biàn怀huái疑yí。”我wǒ疑yí惑huò道dào：“既jì然rán你nǐ不bù认rèn为wéi他tā是shì坏huài人rén，为wèi何hé不bù施shī舍shě？”
6My friend laughed and said: "I don't have any such superpower. I've read those same news reports. As for that old man just now — without evidence, we can't simply suspect him." Confused, I asked: "If you don't think he's a bad person, why didn't you give him anything?"
7 朋péng友yǒu道dào：“可kě我wǒ也yě不bù能néng判pàn断duàn出chū他tā是shì否fǒu真zhēn的de贫pín病bìng无wú依yī，难nán以yǐ自zì食shí其qí力lì啊a。”
7My friend replied: "But I also can't tell whether he is genuinely poor and ill, with no one to rely on, unable to support himself."
8 我wǒ说shuō：“这zhè却què难nán了le。每měi个gè人rén在zài施shī舍shě之zhī前qián，难nán道dào还hái要yào雇gù个gè私sī人rén侦zhēn探tàn，一yī一yī查chá访fǎng乞qǐ丐gài的de收shōu入rù情qíng况kuàng吗ma？”
8I said: "That makes it impossible. Should everyone hire a private detective before giving alms, to investigate each beggar's income one by one?"
9 朋péng友yǒu说shuō：“我wǒ是shì从cóng不bù在zài路lù边biān施shī舍shě的de，因yīn为wèi我wǒ觉jué得de那nà样yàng并bìng不bú是shì仁rén慈cí。当dāng然rán了le，我wǒ不bù敢gǎn说shuō路lù边biān的de每měi一yí个gè人rén都dōu不bù该gāi救jiù助zhù，但dàn救jiù助zhù，也yě要yào看kàn具jù体tǐ情qíng况kuàng。有yǒu些xiē乞qǐ丐gài，你nǐ给gěi他tā一yì点diǎn儿ér钱qián，他tā就jiù叩kòu头tóu。他tā靠kào出chū卖mài尊zūn严yán获huò得dé金jīn钱qián，你nǐ却què靠kào施shī舍shě得de到dào廉lián价jià的de满mǎn足zú。你nǐ的de那nà几jǐ个gè小xiǎo钱qián，是shì不bù配pèi得dé到dào这zhè样yàng的de回huí报bào的de。他tā轻qīng易yì地dì叩kòu头tóu，轻qīng易yì地dì向xiàng人rén展zhǎn示shì生shēng理lǐ残cán疾jí，因yīn为wèi他tā已yǐ不bù看kàn重zhòng自zì我wǒ，也yě不bù尊zūn重zhòng他tā人rén。利lì用yòng丑chǒu恶è博bó得dé金jīn钱qián，自zì古gǔ以yǐ来lái就jiù被bèi称chēng为wéi恶è乞qǐ，被bèi人rén所suǒ不bù齿chǐ。如rú果guǒ你nǐ辛xīn辛xīn苦kǔ苦kǔ挣zhēng来lái的de钱qián，却què助zhù长zhǎng了le不bù良liáng之zhī风fēng，不bù正zhèng与yǔ你nǐ善shàn良liáng的de愿yuàn望wàng背bèi道dào而ér驰chí吗ma？”
9My friend said: "I never give to people on the street, because I don't think it counts as kindness. I'd never say that no one on the street deserves help, but help has to be given with care for the situation. Some beggars, you give them a small amount, and they kowtow. They sell their dignity for money, while you receive cheap satisfaction in exchange for charity. Your few small coins do not deserve a return like that. He kowtows easily, displays his physical disability easily — because he no longer values himself, nor respects others. Using ugliness to gain money has, since ancient times, been called bad begging, and is something people look down on. If your hard-earned money ends up encouraging this kind of bad practice, isn't that the opposite of your kind intentions?"
10 我wǒ随suí即jí又yòu问wèn：“那nà我wǒ们men应yīng该gāi如rú何hé施shī舍shě呢ne？”
10I then asked: "So how should we give?"
11 朋péng友yǒu说shuō: “我wǒ们men应yīng该gāi把bǎ钱qián​​捐juān给gěi慈cí善shàn机jī构gòu，由yóu他tā们men来lái负fù责zé救jiù助zhù，这zhè样yàng可kě以yǐ把bǎ好hǎo钢gāng使shǐ在zài刀dāo刃rèn上shàng，帮bāng助zhù真zhēn正zhèng有yǒu需xū要yào的de人rén。”
11My friend said: "We should give our money to charities, and let them handle the help. That way, we put good steel where it counts most — helping those who genuinely need it."
12 从cóng那nà以yǐ后hòu，我wǒ也yě可kě以yǐ像xiàng朋péng友yǒu一yí样yàng坦tǎn然rán走zǒu过guò乞qǐ丐gài身shēn旁páng。
12From then on, I too could walk past beggars with my friend's calm composure.
