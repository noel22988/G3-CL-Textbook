1 20世shì纪jì初chū，美měi国guó亚yà利lì桑sāng那nà州zhōu北běi部bù有yǒu大dà片piàn茂mào密mì的de森sēn林lín，4000只zhǐ左zuǒ右yòu的de鹿lù在zài林lín间jiān出chū没mò，然rán而ér，鹿lù群qún周zhōu围wéi往wǎng往wǎng会huì有yǒu凶xiōng恶è的de狼láng在zài一yī旁páng窥kuī视shì，许xǔ多duō鹿lù都dōu命mìng丧sàng狼láng口kǒu。美měi国guó总zǒng统tǒng西xī奥ào多duō·罗luó斯sī福fú为wèi了le保bǎo护hù鹿lù，宣xuān布bù该gāi森sēn林lín为wèi全quán国guó狩shòu猎liè保bǎo护hù区qū，由yóu政zhèng府fǔ聘pìn请qǐng猎liè人rén到dào那nà里lǐ去qù灭miè狼láng。
1In the early 20th century, northern Arizona was covered in vast, dense forests. Around 4,000 deer roamed among the trees. But fierce wolves were always watching the deer from the edges, and many deer fell prey to them. US President Theodore Roosevelt, wanting to protect the deer, declared the forest a national hunting reserve, and the government hired hunters to wipe out the wolves.
2 经jīng过guò25年nián的de猎liè捕bǔ，6000多duō只zhǐ狼láng被bèi杀shā死sǐ。由yóu于yú得dé到dào特tè别bié的de保bǎo护hù，森sēn林lín中zhōng鹿lù的de数shù量liàng急jí剧jù上shàng升shēng，总zǒng数shù高gāo达dá10万wàn。在zài这zhè些xiē鹿lù的de啃kěn食shí下xià，森sēn林lín中zhōng的de绿lǜ色sè植zhí物wù一yì天tiān天tiān减jiǎn少shǎo，大dà地dì露lù出chū的de枯kū黄huáng一yì天tiān天tiān扩kuò大dà。终zhōng于yú，灾zāi难nàn降jiàng临lín了le。饥jī饿è、疾jí病bìng使shǐ鹿lù的de数shù量liàng在zài两liǎng年nián内nèi锐ruì减jiǎn到dào4万wàn只zhǐ。到dào1942年nián，整zhěng个gè森sēn林lín只zhǐ剩shèng下xià8000只zhǐ病bìng鹿lù。
2After 25 years of hunting, more than 6,000 wolves had been killed. With special protection, the deer population in the forest skyrocketed to as many as 100,000. Under all that grazing, the greenery in the forest dwindled day by day, and the bare, withered earth grew larger and larger. Eventually, disaster struck. Hunger and disease cut the deer population down to 40,000 in just two years. By 1942, only 8,000 sick deer remained in the entire forest.
3 罗luó斯sī福fú的de护hù鹿lù形xíng同tóng杀shā鹿lù。他tā下xià令lìng捕bǔ杀shā的de恶è狼láng，其qí实shí也yě是shì森sēn林lín的de保bǎo护hù者zhě。尽jǐn管guǎn狼láng吃chī鹿lù，但dàn它tā有yǒu助zhù于yú保bǎo持chí鹿lù群qún的de种zhǒng群qún稳wěn定dìng。因yīn为wèi狼láng吃chī掉diào一yì些xiē鹿lù后hòu，就jiù可kě以yǐ将jiāng森sēn林lín中zhōng鹿lù的de总zǒng数shù控kòng制zhì在zài一yí个gè合hé理lǐ的de程chéng度dù，森sēn林lín也yě就jiù不bú会huì被bèi鹿lù群qún糟zāo蹋tà得dé面miàn目mù全quán非fēi。同tóng时shí，狼láng吃chī掉diào的de多duō数shù是shì病bìng鹿lù，这zhè能néng有yǒu效xiào控kòng制zhì疾jí病bìng对duì鹿lù群qún的de威wēi胁xié。鹿lù的de数shù量liàng一yí旦dàn超chāo过guò森sēn林lín可kě以yǐ承chéng受shòu的de程chéng度dù，就jiù会huì破pò坏huài生shēng态tài系xì统tǒng的de平píng衡héng，给gěi森sēn林lín带dài来lái巨jù大dà的de灾zāi难nàn。也yě就jiù是shì说shuō，过guò多duō的de鹿lù会huì成chéng为wéi毁huǐ灭miè森sēn林lín的de罪zuì魁kuí祸huò首shǒu。
3Roosevelt's protection of the deer ended up amounting to their slaughter. The "vicious" wolves he ordered killed had actually been the forest's protectors. Yes, wolves eat deer — but in doing so, they help keep deer populations stable. Each deer a wolf takes helps keep the total population at a sustainable level, so the forest doesn't get gnawed to ruin. What's more, the deer wolves take are mostly sick ones, which effectively limits the spread of disease through the herd. Once the deer population exceeds what the forest can sustain, the ecosystem's balance breaks down, and disaster strikes. In other words, too many deer become the very thing that destroys the forest.
4 动dòng物wù学xué家jiā把bǎ这zhè种zhǒng与yǔ天tiān敌dí共gòng荣róng的de现xiàn象xiàng称chēng之zhī为wèi狼láng鹿lù效xiào应yìng。那nà么me，为wèi什shén么me会huì产chǎn生shēng狼láng鹿lù效xiào应yìng呢ne？
4Zoologists call this phenomenon — thriving alongside one's natural predator — the "wolf-deer effect." So why does the wolf-deer effect happen?
5 因yīn为wèi狼láng和hé鹿lù是shì生shēng态tài链liàn上shàng紧jǐn密mì相xiāng连lián的de两liǎng环huán，两liǎng者zhě之zhī间jiān通tōng过guò竞jìng争zhēng、冲chōng突tū与yǔ周zhōu围wéi的de生shēng态tài环huán境jìng取qǔ得dé平píng衡héng。
5Because wolves and deer are two tightly-linked rings of the ecological chain. Through competition and conflict between them, both find equilibrium with the wider ecosystem.
6 为wèi了le证zhèng实shí这zhè个ge原yuán理lǐ，美měi国guó生shēng物wù学xué家jiā在zài一yí个gè小xiǎo岛dǎo上shàng进jìn行xíng了le长zhǎng达dá32年nián的de考kǎo察chá研yán究jiū。在zài那nà里lǐ，他tā亲qīn眼yǎn目mù睹dǔ了le这zhè两liǎng种zhǒng动dòng物wù和hé森sēn林lín间jiān的de生shēng态tài平píng衡héng关guān系xì。作zuò为wéi鹿lù的de天tiān敌dí，狼láng一yī有yǒu机jī会huì就jiù发fā动dòng袭xí击jī。老lǎo弱ruò的de鹿lù难nán以yǐ逃táo脱tuō，往wǎng往wǎng被bèi狼láng猎liè杀shā，身shēn躯qū强qiáng壮zhuàng的de鹿lù则zé有yǒu更gèng多duō机jī会huì生shēng存cún下xià来lái。没méi有yǒu了le狼láng，鹿lù虽suī能néng在zài短duǎn时shí间jiān内nèi迅xùn速sù繁fán殖zhí，最zuì终zhōng却què会huì因yīn饥jī饿è和hé疾jí病bìng大dà批pī死sǐ去qù。反fǎn之zhī，如rú果guǒ少shǎo了le鹿lù，狼láng也yě会huì因yīn得dé不bú到dào足zú够gòu的de食shí物wù而ér无wú法fǎ生shēng存cún。
6To confirm this principle, an American biologist conducted a 32-year-long study on a small island. There, he saw with his own eyes the ecological balance between these two animals and their forest. As the deer's natural predator, the wolf attacks whenever it gets the chance. The old and weak struggle to escape and are often hunted down, while the strong have a better chance of surviving. Without wolves, deer can multiply rapidly in the short term — but eventually large numbers die off from hunger and disease. Conversely, if there are too few deer, the wolves can't get enough food and cannot survive either.
7 “灭miè狼láng护hù鹿lù”的de举jǔ措cuò违wéi背bèi了le自zì然rán界jiè的de法fǎ则zé，最zuì终zhōng给gěi鹿lù群qún带dài来lái了le更gèng大dà的de灾zāi难nàn。
7The strategy of "killing wolves to protect deer" violated nature's own laws — and in the end, brought the deer themselves a far greater disaster.
8 狼láng鹿lù效xiào应yìng不bù仅jǐn适shì用yòng于yú自zì然rán界jiè，也yě同tóng样yàng适shì用yòng于yú人rén类lèi社shè会huì。在zài现xiàn今jīn这zhè个ge充chōng满mǎn竞jìng争zhēng的de社shè会huì，追zhuī求qiú成chéng功gōng的de人rén非fēi常cháng需xū要yào强qiáng劲jìng的de竞jìng争zhēng对duì手shǒu。竞jìng争zhēng对duì手shǒu的de存cún在zài，会huì激jī发fā一yí个gè人rén的de拼pīn搏bó意yì识shí。双shuāng方fāng的de关guān系xì如rú同tóng狼láng鹿lù一yí样yàng，互hù相xiāng依yī存cún，缺quē一yī不bù可kě。
8The wolf-deer effect applies not just to the natural world, but to human society as well. In today's intensely competitive society, those who pursue success badly need formidable rivals. The presence of a competitor sparks the will to fight in the other person. The two are like wolf and deer — mutually dependent, neither one able to do without the other.
