《世界各地的新年风俗》 New year customs around the world

语速 Speed 0.9× (set before play)
西xī班bān牙yá
午wǔ夜yè葡pú萄táo当dāng教jiào堂táng的de钟zhōng声shēng在zài午wǔ夜yè12点diǎn响xiǎng起qǐ时shí，大dà家jiā都dōu争zhēng着zhe吃chī葡pú萄táo，如rú果guǒ能néng跟gēn着zhe钟zhōng声shēng连lián续xù吃chī下xià12颗kē，就jiù象xiàng征zhēng新xīn的de一yī年nián12个gè月yuè都dōu事shì事shì如rú意yì。
Spain
Midnight grapes. When the church bells ring at midnight, everyone rushes to eat grapes. Eating all 12 in time with the chimes symbolises a year in which every month goes well.
古gǔ巴bā
泼pō水shuǐ迎yíng新xīn按àn照zhào当dāng地dì风fēng俗sú，每měi人rén准zhǔn备bèi一yī碗wǎn清qīng水shuǐ，等děng午wǔ夜yè钟zhōng声shēng敲qiāo响xiǎng后hòu，各gè自zì把bǎ水shuǐ泼pō到dào窗chuāng外wài，以yǐ示shì辞cí旧jiù迎yíng新xīn。
Cuba
Water out the window. By local custom, each person prepares a bowl of clean water. When the midnight bells ring, they toss the water out the window — bidding farewell to the old year and welcoming the new.
意yì大dà利lì
扔rēng旧jiù迎yíng新xīn夜yè幕mù降jiàng临lín，人rén们men在zài街jiē头tóu燃rán放fàng烟yān火huǒ，尽jìn情qíng起qǐ舞wǔ。到dào了le午wǔ夜yè时shí分fēn，家jiā家jiā户hù户hù把bǎ旧jiù的de瓶píng瓶píng罐guàn罐guàn扔rēng出chū窗chuāng外wài，表biǎo示shì去qù除chú烦fán恼nǎo和hé厄è运yùn。
Italy
Throwing out the old. As night falls, people set off fireworks and dance in the streets. At midnight, every household tosses old jars and bottles out of the window to cast away worries and bad luck.
丹dān麦mài
碎suì盘pán传chuán情qíng新xīn年nián前qián夕xī，人rén们men把bǎ平píng时shí打dǎ碎suì的de杯bēi盘pán碗wǎn碟dié偷tōu偷tōu送sòng到dào朋péng友yǒu家jiā的de门mén前qián。谁shuí家jiā堆duī放fàng的de碎suì片piàn多duō，就jiù说shuō明míng谁shuí家jiā的de朋péng友yǒu多duō，也yě预yù示shì着zhe来lái年nián的de好hǎo兆zhào头tou。
Denmark
Broken plates at the door. On New Year's Eve, people quietly carry broken cups, plates and bowls to their friends' doorsteps. The bigger the pile on your doorstep, the more friends you have — and the better your luck in the year ahead.
世shì界jiè各gè地dì的de华huá人rén
除chú尘chén辞cí旧jiù农nóng历lì新xīn年nián来lái临lín前qián，华huá人rén会huì在zài家jiā里lǐ大dà扫sǎo除chú，辞cí旧jiù迎yíng新xīn。
团tuán圆yuán饭fàn除chú夕xī夜yè，不bù管guǎn工gōng作zuò多duō忙máng碌lù，路lù途tú多duō遥yáo远yuǎn，大dà家jiā都dōu要yào回huí家jiā团tuán聚jù，开kāi开kāi心xīn心xīn地dì围wéi坐zuò在zài一yì起qǐ吃chī团tuán圆yuán饭fàn。
拜bài年nián大dà年nián初chū一yī，人rén们men恭gōng敬jìng地dì给gěi长zhǎng辈bèi和hé亲qīn戚qī拜bài年nián，互hù相xiāng祝zhù福fú，希xī望wàng在zài新xīn的de一yī年nián身shēn体tǐ健jiàn康kāng，万wàn事shì大dà吉jí。
Chinese communities around the world
Spring-cleaning. Before Lunar New Year arrives, Chinese families do a thorough spring-cleaning of their homes — sending off the old year to welcome the new.
The reunion dinner. On New Year's Eve, no matter how busy work is or how far the journey home, everyone returns to gather with family and share a reunion dinner together.
Paying New Year visits. On the first day of the new year, people respectfully visit elders and relatives to exchange greetings — wishing each other good health and good fortune in the year ahead.
小xiǎo知zhī识shí：春chūn节jié和hé元yuán旦dàn
别bié名míng农nóng历lì新xīn年nián也yě叫jiào“春chūn节jié”。
两liǎng个gè新xīn年nián元yuán旦dàn是shì阳yáng历lì新xīn年nián的de第dì一yī天tiān，即jí一yí月yuè一yī日rì。春chūn节jié是shì农nóng历lì新xīn年nián的de第dì一yī天tiān，即jí正zhēng月yuè初chū一yī。
A note: 春节 vs 元旦
Another name. Lunar New Year is also called 春节 (Chūnjié), the Spring Festival.
Two different new years. 元旦 (Yuándàn) is the first day of the solar calendar — January 1st. 春节 (Chūnjié) is the first day of the lunar calendar, which falls on a different date each year.
