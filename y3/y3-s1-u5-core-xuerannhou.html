1 雄xióng鹰yīng只zhǐ有yǒu在zài飞fēi上shàng蓝lán天tiān后hòu，才cái知zhī道dào天tiān空kōng是shì多duō么me广guǎng阔kuò，自zì己jǐ是shì多duō么me渺miǎo小xiǎo；小xiǎo河hé只zhǐ有yǒu在zài奔bēn入rù大dà海hǎi时shí，才cái知zhī道dào海hǎi洋yáng是shì多duō么me宽kuān广guǎng，自zì己jǐ是shì多duō么me微wēi不bù足zú道dào；坐zuò井jǐng观guān天tiān、自zì鸣míng得dé意yì的de青qīng蛙wā只zhǐ有yǒu从cóng井jǐng里lǐ跳tiào出chū来lái，才cái知zhī道dào世shì界jiè是shì多duō么me精jīng彩cǎi，自zì己jǐ是shì多duō么me孤gū陋lòu寡guǎ闻wén。俗sú话huà说shuō：“学xué然rán后hòu知zhī不bù足zú。”一yí个gè人rén学xué得dé越yuè多duō，越yuè会huì发fā现xiàn自zì己jǐ学xué到dào的de知zhī识shí只zhǐ是shì九jiǔ牛niú一yì毛máo，也yě会huì越yuè明míng白bái学xué海hǎi无wú涯yá，应yīng该gāi不bù断duàn进jìn取qǔ的de道dào理lǐ。
1Only after soaring into the blue does the eagle realise how vast the sky is, and how tiny it really is. Only after rushing into the sea does the stream discover how boundless the ocean is, and how insignificant it is in turn. Only after leaping out of its well does the smug little frog, forever gazing at its little patch of sky, discover how wonderful the world truly is, and how ignorant it has been. As the saying goes, "To learn is to see how much you don't know." The more a person learns, the more they realise their knowledge is but a drop in the ocean — and the more they understand that learning has no limits, and that one must keep pressing forward.
2 有yǒu些xiē人rén不bù愿yuàn意yì深shēn入rù学xué习xí，这zhè样yàng当dāng然rán就jiù与yǔ未wèi知zhī的de领lǐng域yù接jiē触chù得dé少shǎo，也yě就jiù不bù知zhī道dào未wèi知zhī的de领lǐng域yù是shì多duō么me深shēn远yuǎn奥ào妙miào。这zhè些xiē人rén自zì然rán不bú会huì意yì识shí到dào自zì己jǐ的de不bù足zú，反fǎn而ér还hái以yǐ为wéi自zì己jǐ很hěn有yǒu学xué问wèn。中zhōng国guó古gǔ代dài有yǒu一yī则zé寓yù言yán，讲jiǎng的de是shì一yí个gè楚chǔ国guó人rén学xué习xí划huá船chuán的de故gù事shì。一yī开kāi始shǐ，他tā完wán全quán听tīng从cóng师shī傅fù的de教jiào导dǎo，在zài江jiāng中zhōng小xiǎo岛dǎo之zhī间jiān尝cháng试shì划huá船chuán，感gǎn到dào得dé心xīn应yìng手shǒu。他tā以yǐ为wéi自zì己jǐ已yǐ经jīng完wán全quán掌zhǎng握wò了le划huá船chuán技jì术shù，就jiù得dé意yì洋yáng洋yáng，不bù久jiǔ便biàn谢xiè别bié了le师shī傅fù。谁shuí知zhī当dāng他tā独dú自zì划huá船chuán前qián行xíng时shí，遇yù到dào了le危wēi险xiǎn。他tā惊jīng慌huāng失shī措cuò，四sì处chù张zhāng望wàng，吓xià得dé连lián船chuán桨jiǎng都dōu掉diào进jìn了le江jiāng中zhōng。学xué习xí不bù能néng浅qiǎn尝cháng辄zhé止zhǐ，那nà名míng楚chǔ国guó人rén若ruò能néng深shēn入rù地dì学xué习xí划huá船chuán的de技jì术shù，遇yù到dào危wēi险xiǎn时shí又yòu怎zěn么me会huì如rú此cǐ慌huāng乱luàn？
2Some people are reluctant to learn deeply. Naturally, they have little contact with the unknown, and so never discover how profound and mysterious the unknown can be. Such people don't realise their shortcomings — in fact, they think themselves very learned. There's an ancient Chinese fable about a man from the state of Chu who learned to row a boat. At first, he followed his master's instructions faithfully, practised between the small river islands, and found it came easily. Thinking he had already mastered the art, he grew conceited and soon bid his master farewell. Who would have guessed that, rowing alone, he ran into danger. Seized by panic, he looked wildly around, so frightened that his oars slipped into the river. Learning cannot stop at the shallow end — had that man from Chu learned the craft of rowing more deeply, how could he have panicked so badly at the first sign of danger?
3 相xiāng反fǎn的de，越yuè是shì深shēn入rù学xué习xí，就jiù越yuè能néng感gǎn受shòu到dào自zì己jǐ掌zhǎng握wò的de知zhī识shí有yǒu限xiàn。古gǔ希xī腊là著zhù名míng哲zhé学xué家jiā芝zhī诺nuò曾céng经jīng用yòng一yí个gè生shēng动dòng的de比bǐ喻yù来lái说shuō明míng这zhè个ge道dào理lǐ。他tā用yòng一yí个gè圆yuán的de内nèi部bù来lái表biǎo示shì自zì己jǐ已yǐ掌zhǎng握wò的de知zhī识shí，而ér用yòng圆yuán的de外wài部bù表biǎo示shì未wèi知zhī的de知zhī识shí。他tā说shuō一yí个gè人rén知zhī识shí越yuè多duō，这zhè个ge圆yuán就jiù越yuè大dà，与yǔ未wèi知zhī领lǐng域yù的de接jiē触chù也yě越yuè多duō。因yīn此cǐ，他tā对duì学xué生shēng说shuō：“从cóng这zhè个ge意yì义yì上shàng讲jiǎng，我wǒ不bù知zhī道dào的de东dōng西xī比bǐ你nǐ们men更gèng多duō。”芝zhī诺nuò意yì识shí到dào自zì己jǐ不bù知zhī道dào的de事shì情qíng很hěn多duō，所suǒ以yǐ不bù断duàn进jìn取qǔ，最zuì后hòu在zài学xué术shù上shàng取qǔ得dé了le辉huī煌huáng的de成chéng就jiù。
3On the other hand, the deeper you learn, the more you sense how limited your own knowledge is. The ancient Greek philosopher Zeno once illustrated this with a vivid metaphor. He used the inside of a circle to represent the knowledge one has mastered, and the outside of the circle to represent the knowledge one has yet to learn. The more a person knows, he said, the larger this circle grows — and the larger the boundary that touches the unknown. So he told his students: "In this sense, I know more about what I don't know than you do." Realising just how much he didn't know, Zeno kept pushing forward, and ultimately achieved brilliant results in his scholarship.
4 学xué然rán后hòu知zhī不bù足zú，首shǒu先xiān必bì须xū学xué，要yào对duì学xué习xí充chōng满mǎn热rè忱chén，并bìng且qiě持chí之zhī以yǐ恒héng。只zhǐ有yǒu这zhè样yàng，才cái能néng获huò得dé更gèng多duō的de知zhī识shí，才cái能néng意yì识shí到dào自zì己jǐ的de不bù足zú；也yě只zhǐ有yǒu永yǒng不bù满mǎn足zú，才cái能néng激jī励lì自zì己jǐ不bù断duàn学xué习xí，不bù断duàn进jìn取qǔ。古gǔ今jīn中zhōng外wài，不bù同tóng领lǐng域yù的de名míng人rén都dōu曾céng经jīng说shuō过guò类lèi似sì的de道dào理lǐ。中zhōng国guó文wén学xué家jiā鲁lǔ迅xùn说shuō：“不bù满mǎn足zú是shì向xiàng上shàng的de车chē轮lún。”法fǎ国guó哲zhé学xué家jiā笛dí卡kǎ尔ěr说shuō：“我wǒ的de努nǔ力lì求qiú学xué没méi有yǒu得dé到dào别bié的de好hǎo处chù，只zhǐ不bù过guò是shì越yuè来lái越yuè发fā觉jué自zì己jǐ的de无wú知zhī。”这zhè些xiē名míng人rén从cóng不bù同tóng角jiǎo度dù告gào诉sù我wǒ们men，要yào永yǒng不bù满mǎn足zú，不bù断duàn进jìn取qǔ。
4To "learn and then know one's shortcomings," one must first learn — with enthusiasm, and with perseverance over time. Only then can we gain more knowledge and recognise how much we still lack. And only by never being satisfied can we push ourselves to keep learning, keep advancing. Famous thinkers through the ages, in every field, have said much the same thing. The Chinese writer Lu Xun said, "Dissatisfaction is the wheel that drives us upward." The French philosopher Descartes said, "My pursuit of learning has given me no other reward than to discover my ignorance more and more." These figures, each from their own angle, tell us the same thing: never be satisfied — always press on.
5 宋sòng朝cháo思sī想xiǎng家jiā朱zhū熹xī在zài一yī首shǒu诗shī中zhōng写xiě道dào：“问wèn渠qú哪nǎ得dé清qīng如rú许xǔ，为wèi有yǒu源yuán头tóu活huó水shuǐ来lái。”这zhè句jù诗shī的de意yì思si是shì：为wèi什shén么me池chí水shuǐ会huì这zhè样yàng清qīng澈chè呢ne？是shì因yīn为wèi有yǒu永yǒng不bù枯kū竭jié的de源yuán头tóu为wèi它tā源yuán源yuán不bù断duàn地dì输shū送sòng活huó水shuǐ。如rú果guǒ把bǎ知zhī识shí比bǐ作zuò清qīng水shuǐ，那nà么me持chí续xù不bú断duàn的de学xué习xí就jiù是shì永yǒng不bù枯kū竭jié的de源yuán头tóu。只zhǐ有yǒu永yǒng不bù满mǎn足zú地dì学xué习xí，才cái能néng增zēng进jìn知zhī识shí，使shǐ知zhī识shí的de源yuán泉quán永yǒng不bù干gàn涸hé，永yǒng远yuǎn流liú淌tǎng着zhe清qīng澈chè的de泉quán水shuǐ，浇jiāo灌guàn智zhì慧huì的de花huā朵duǒ，结jié出chū甜tián美měi的de果guǒ实shí。
5The Song-dynasty thinker Zhu Xi once wrote: "Ask the pond how it stays so clear — it is because there is a fresh spring that never runs dry at its source." What these lines mean is this: why is the pond water so clear? Because an ever-flowing source keeps sending in fresh water without end. If knowledge is like clear water, then continuous learning is the never-drying spring that feeds it. Only by learning without ever being satisfied can we deepen our knowledge, keep the wellspring of knowing from running dry, and let its clear streams water the flowers of wisdom, bearing sweet fruit in the end.
