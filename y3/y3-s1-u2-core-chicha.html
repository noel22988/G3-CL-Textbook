1 在zài很hěn多duō华huá人rén生shēng活huó的de地dì方fāng，人rén们men把bǎ喝hē茶chá称chēng为wéi“吃chī茶chá”。人rén们men冲chōng泡pào茶chá叶yè后hòu明míng明míng是shì在zài喝hē茶chá，为wèi什shén么me要yào称chēng为wéi“吃chī茶chá”呢ne？其qí实shí，在zài古gǔ代dài，人rén们men饮yǐn茶chá时shí的dí确què是shì把bǎ茶chá叶yè一yì起qǐ“吃chī”下xià去qù的de。
1In many parts of the Chinese-speaking world, people call drinking tea "eating tea" (吃茶). They're clearly drinking the brewed leaves — why call it eating? As it turns out, in ancient times, people really did eat the tea leaves along with the water.
2 茶chá的de历lì史shǐ源yuán远yuǎn流liú长cháng。传chuán说shuō在zài上shàng古gǔ时shí期qī，华huá人rén最zuì早zǎo的de祖zǔ先xiān之zhī一yī神shén农nóng氏shì，曾céng在zài尝cháng试shì药yào草cǎo功gōng效xiào时shí中zhòng毒dú，幸xìng亏kuī有yǒu茶chá叶yè解jiě毒dú才cái能néng够gòu脱tuō险xiǎn。虽suī然rán这zhè只zhǐ是shì一yí个gè传chuán说shuō，但dàn可kě以yǐ看kàn出chū茶chá很hěn早zǎo就jiù已yǐ经jīng进jìn入rù人rén们men的de生shēng活huó。
2Tea has a long, long history. Legend has it that in ancient times, Shennong — one of the earliest ancestors of the Chinese people — was poisoned while testing the effects of medicinal herbs, and was saved when tea leaves neutralised the poison. Though just a legend, it shows how early tea had already entered people's lives.
3 从cóng两liǎng千qiān年nián前qián的de西xī汉hàn时shí期qī（公gōng元yuán前qián206-公gōng元yuán25年nián）开kāi始shǐ，茶chá逐zhú渐jiàn成chéng为wéi一yī种zhǒng饮yǐn品pǐn。当dāng时shí，人rén们men把bǎ茶chá叶yè放fàng在zài锅guō里lǐ，加jiā水shuǐ煮zhǔ成chéng浓nóng汤tāng，再zài盛shèng到dào碗wǎn里lǐ饮yǐn用yòng。
3From the Western Han dynasty (206 BCE – 25 CE), two thousand years ago, tea gradually became a drink. At the time, people would put tea leaves in a pot, add water, boil it into a thick broth, and then ladle it into bowls to drink.
4 经jīng过guò几jǐ百bǎi年nián的de发fā展zhǎn，到dào了le唐táng代dài（公gōng元yuán618-907年nián），饮yǐn茶chá的de风fēng气qì逐zhú渐jiàn盛shèng行xíng，人rén们men会huì先xiān把bǎ茶chá叶yè磨mó成chéng粉fěn末mò，在zài煮zhǔ茶chá时shí还hái会huì加jiā入rù葱cōng、姜jiāng、橘jú子zi皮pí等děng调tiáo味wèi料liào，饮yǐn用yòng时shí连lián茶chá叶yè粉fěn末mò一yì起qǐ喝hē下xià去qù。
4After several centuries of development, by the Tang dynasty (618–907 CE), tea-drinking was becoming widespread. People would first grind the tea leaves into powder, and when boiling the tea they'd add seasonings like spring onion, ginger, and tangerine peel — drinking it all down, powdered leaves included.
5 饮yǐn茶chá的de风fēng气qì在zài宋sòng代dài（公gōng元yuán960-1279年nián）达dá到dào鼎dǐng盛shèng。这zhè一yì时shí期qī，茶chá已yǐ经jīng成chéng为wéi日rì常cháng生shēng活huó中zhōng的de必bì需xū品pǐn，正zhèng所suǒ谓wèi“开kāi门mén七qī件jiàn事shì：柴chái米mǐ油yóu盐yán酱jiàng醋cù茶chá”。宋sòng代dài人rén说shuō起qǐ喝hē茶chá，都dōu习xí惯guàn称chēng为wéi“吃chī茶chá”。他tā们men一yì般bān会huì将jiāng茶chá叶yè磨mó成chéng粉fěn末mò，放fàng入rù茶chá碗wǎn，倒dào入rù少shǎo量liàng沸fèi水shuǐ调tiáo匀yún，接jiē着zhe加jiā水shuǐ，大dà力lì搅jiǎo拌bàn后hòu饮yǐn用yòng。
5Tea-drinking reached its peak in the Song dynasty (960–1279 CE). By this period tea had become a household essential — as the saying goes, "the seven things you open your door to: firewood, rice, oil, salt, sauce, vinegar, and tea." Song-dynasty people habitually called drinking tea "eating tea." They would typically grind the leaves into powder, put it in a tea bowl, pour in a little boiling water and mix it smooth, add more water, whisk it vigorously, and then drink it.
6 到dào了le明míng代dài（公gōng元yuán1368-1644年nián），饮yǐn茶chá进jìn一yí步bù普pǔ及jí。人rén们men通tōng常cháng用yòng沸fèi水shuǐ冲chōng泡pào茶chá叶yè，饮yǐn用yòng时shí只zhǐ喝hē茶chá水shuǐ，泡pào过guò的de茶chá叶yè则zé弃qì之zhī不bù用yòng。
6By the Ming dynasty (1368–1644 CE), tea-drinking had spread even further. People now typically brewed tea by steeping leaves in boiling water, drinking only the liquid and discarding the used leaves.
7 时shí至zhì今jīn日rì，茶chá已yǐ经jīng成chéng为wéi世shì界jiè性xìng的de饮yǐn品pǐn。人rén们men喝hē茶chá的de方fāng式shì越yuè来lái越yuè多duō样yàng化huà，在zài各gè式shì各gè样yàng的de茶chá饮yǐn料liào中zhōng，泡pào泡pào茶chá异yì军jūn突tū起qǐ。上shàng世shì纪jì90年nián代dài，新xīn加jiā坡pō开kāi始shǐ出chū现xiàn泡pào泡pào茶chá店diàn。当dāng时shí，人rén们men用yòng玻bō璃lí杯bēi来lái装zhuāng泡pào泡pào茶chá，顾gù客kè只zhǐ能néng在zài店diàn里lǐ享xiǎng用yòng。进jìn入rù21世shì纪jì，外wài卖mài式shì的de泡pào泡pào茶chá店diàn开kāi始shǐ流liú行xíng起qǐ来lái。随suí着zhe泡pào泡pào茶chá店diàn越yuè开kāi越yuè多duō，泡pào泡pào茶chá的de种zhǒng类lèi也yě变biàn得dé五wǔ花huā八bā门mén，有yǒu珍zhēn珠zhū奶nǎi茶chá，有yǒu冷lěng泡pào茶chá，还hái有yǒu口kǒu味wèi多duō样yàng的de果guǒ茶chá等děng。
7Today, tea has become a worldwide drink. The ways people enjoy it have grown ever more varied, and among all the modern tea drinks, bubble tea has surged ahead. Bubble tea shops began appearing in Singapore in the 1990s. Back then, drinks were served in glass cups for dine-in only. Entering the 21st century, takeaway-style bubble tea shops started to take off. As more shops opened, the varieties multiplied — pearl milk tea, cold-brewed tea, all kinds of fruit teas, and more.
8 如rú果guǒ说shuō喝hē茶chá是shì为wèi了le解jiě渴kě，那nà么me品pǐn茶chá则zé是shì一yī种zhǒng艺yì术shù。三sān五wǔ好hǎo友yǒu一yì起qǐ品pǐn茶chá时shí，一yì般bān都dōu包bāo括kuò这zhè几jǐ个gè步bù骤zhòu。首shǒu先xiān是shì“布bù具jù”。就jiù是shì把bǎ茶chá壶hú、茶chá杯bēi、茶chá罐guàn等děng器qì具jù准zhǔn备bèi好hǎo。其qí次cì是shì“赏shǎng茶chá”，从cóng茶chá罐guàn中zhōng取qǔ出chū适shì量liàng的de茶chá叶yè，鉴jiàn赏shǎng干gàn茶chá的de外wài形xíng和hé颜yán色sè。接jiē下xià来lái是shì“冲chōng泡pào与yǔ奉fèng茶chá”。先xiān把bǎ茶chá叶yè放fàng入rù茶chá壶hú，注zhù入rù适shì量liàng开kāi水shuǐ，稍shāo等děng片piàn刻kè，就jiù可kě以yǐ将jiāng泡pào好hǎo的de茶chá倒dào入rù茶chá杯bēi，再zài双shuāng手shǒu捧pěng起qǐ，递dì给gěi客kè人rén。最zuì后hòu是shì“饮yǐn茶chá”。先xiān闻wén闻wén茶chá香xiāng，观guān赏shǎng茶chá水shuǐ的de颜yán色sè，然rán后hòu小xiǎo口kǒu品pǐn尝cháng，充chōng分fèn体tǐ会huì茶chá的de滋zī味wèi和hé香xiāng气qì。
8If drinking tea is about quenching thirst, then appreciating tea is an art. When a few friends gather to savour tea together, the process usually goes through several steps. First is "setting out the tools" — laying out the teapot, cups, and tea caddy. Next is "admiring the tea" — taking a measured amount of leaves from the caddy and appreciating their shape and colour. Then comes "brewing and serving": placing the leaves in the pot, pouring in the right amount of hot water, waiting a moment, then pouring the brewed tea into the cups and offering them to the guests with both hands. Finally, there is "drinking" — first inhaling the aroma, then admiring the colour, and only then sipping slowly to fully appreciate the taste and fragrance.
9 从cóng古gǔ至zhì今jīn，人rén们men都dōu认rèn为wéi茶chá对duì健jiàn康kāng有yǒu益yì。古gǔ人rén认rèn为wéi，茶chá有yǒu保bǎo健jiàn功gōng能néng，喝hē茶chá不bù但dàn让ràng人rén生shēng津jīn止zhǐ渴kě，还hái可kě以yǐ消xiāo暑shǔ清qīng热rè。现xiàn代dài医yī学xué也yě发fā现xiàn，茶chá叶yè中zhōng的de成chéng分fèn有yǒu些xiē能néng起qǐ到dào提tí神shén醒xǐng脑nǎo的de作zuò用yòng，有yǒu些xiē则zé具jù有yǒu抗kàng菌jūn消xiāo炎yán的de效xiào果guǒ。
9From ancient times to today, tea has been considered good for health. The ancients believed tea had medicinal benefits: it not only quenches thirst but also cools the body in the summer heat. Modern medicine has confirmed that compounds in tea can help sharpen the mind, while others have antibacterial and anti-inflammatory effects.
10 茶chá已yǐ经jīng成chéng为wéi我wǒ们men生shēng活huó中zhōng的de一yī部bù分fèn。下xià次cì当dāng你nǐ端duān起qǐ一yī杯bēi茶chá时shí，不bù妨fáng想xiǎng一yī想xiǎng，这zhè杯bēi茶chá可kě以yǐ告gào诉sù我wǒ们men怎zěn样yàng的de故gù事shì。
10Tea has become part of our lives. Next time you pick up a cup, take a moment to wonder: what stories might this cup have to tell?
